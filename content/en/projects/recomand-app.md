About three years ago I needed a tradesperson. An ordinary job, nothing exotic. I opened my contact list and started calling.

By the seventh call I realised I had told exactly the same story seven times: what needed doing, where, roughly when, roughly what budget. Seven people, seven repetitions of the same description, three "I'll call you back" that never came, and a single concrete price — with nothing to compare it against.

The problem wasn't that there were no tradespeople. There were. The problem was that **the effort of describing the job fell entirely on me, and it multiplied with every supplier I wanted to ask.**

That's how [Recomand](https://recomand.app) began.

## The idea: describe it once, the offers come to you

The whole platform rests on a single inversion of the flow.

Instead of you hunting for tradespeople and repeating the story to each one, **you describe the job once**, and the request reaches the professionals who actually work in your area, on your service. They come to you with prices and timelines.

[Three steps](https://recomand.app/cum-functioneaza), from the client's side:

1. **[Describe the job](https://recomand.app/cerere)** — pick the service, say what you need, where and roughly when. It takes under two minutes and you don't need an account beforehand.
2. **Receive offers** — the request reaches professionals in your area, who send a price, a timeline and details.
3. **Compare and choose** — see the offers side by side, with reviews from previous clients, discuss the details and choose.

The subtle but important difference: because every offer starts from **the same description**, they are comparable. You're not comparing apples to oranges, because every tradesperson read exactly the same text.

## What Recomand is today

It isn't a concept on paper. It's a working platform:

- **217 distinct services**, grouped into **9 categories** — from [renovations and interior work](https://recomand.app/categorii/amenajari-interioare) (the largest category, 92 services) and [installations, fitting and repairs](https://recomand.app/categorii/montaj-si-reparatii) (63 services), through to [cleaning](https://recomand.app/categorii/curatenie), [moving and transport](https://recomand.app/categorii/mutari), [health and personal care](https://recomand.app/categorii/infrumusetare-sanatate), [business services](https://recomand.app/categorii/afaceri), [lessons and tutoring](https://recomand.app/categorii/lectii), [events](https://recomand.app/categorii/evenimente) and [legal](https://recomand.app/categorii/justitie).
- Coverage across **41 counties** and more than **13,000 localities** in Romania.
- **Moderation on every request** before it reaches professionals — the filter that keeps spam and incomplete requests away from tradespeople.
- **Validated accounts** for professionals: nobody sends an offer before passing the validation process.
- **Real reviews**, tied to real jobs on the platform, which stay attached to the profile.
- **Your phone number visible only to the professionals who bid** — it doesn't turn into a contact list to be resold.
- **Two mobile apps**, with real-time notifications:
  - 🔗 *Recomand* (clients) — [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=client.recomand.app)
  - 🔗 *Recomand Pro* (professionals) — [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=ro.recomand.app) · [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/ro/app/recomand-pro/id6446204407)

For clients, posting is free and there is no commission on the value of the job. You are under no obligation to accept any offer.

For professionals, [the model runs on credits](https://recomand.app/devino-partener): **you pay only for the requests you choose to open.** No mandatory subscription, no commission on the job. You pick the services and the localities you work in, and you only receive requests that match. You see the request summary — description, deadline, locality — before you spend a credit.

I cared a great deal about this. A good tradesperson shouldn't pay a monthly subscription for the privilege of receiving nothing in the slow months, and shouldn't hand over a percentage of a job he carried out himself, with his own hands. He pays for access to the information, that's all.

## The part nobody usually writes: I abandoned it

I built the product. And then I let it sit.

Not because it didn't work. Because **I knew how to build the product, but I didn't know how to promote it.**

A marketplace has a problem an ordinary product doesn't: it's empty until you fill it from both sides at once. Clients don't come if there are no tradespeople to answer. Tradespeople don't stay if there are no requests. You can have the best technical execution on the market — if you don't solve the *cold start*, you have a beautiful, empty database.

At that point I didn't have the answer. I preferred to admit it rather than burn budgets on badly made ads, and I put the project on hold.

In hindsight, it was the right call. The mistake wasn't the pause. The mistake would have been insisting with know-how I didn't have.

## What changed in the meantime

The years that passed weren't a pause, they were school.

I worked on distribution, on automation, on programmatic SEO, on AI integrations, and on products taken from zero to real users. Among other things, I learned:

- that **content at scale** is the natural weapon of a marketplace with 217 services × 13,000 localities — every service-city combination is a real search intent, and the platform's structure already supports it;
- that the hard part isn't traffic, it's **liquidity**: the ratio between requests and active tradespeople, for each service-area pair. It's solved geographically, city by city, not nationally all at once;
- that **acquiring professionals** is a sales operation, not a marketing one, and it works differently from acquiring clients;
- that automation and AI make possible, for one person today, a volume of execution that three years ago would have taken me a team.

In short: **the product was ready before I was.** That's no longer the case.
